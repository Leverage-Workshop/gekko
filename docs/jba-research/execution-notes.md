# Execution Notes

Evidence log for the **execution** side of the method — how a plan gets traded once the session is
live. Companion to [Execution Process](./execution-process.md), which is the derived rule set.

Separate from the planning research (`jba-prep-video-notes.md` / `jba-analysis-process.md`) on
purpose. Planning is *where and what if*; execution is *which, when, and how much*. The prep videos
answer the first and are nearly silent on the second.

**Corpus: 0 trade-replay videos analyzed.** Nothing below is derived from a replay yet.

**Tagging:** `[stated]` · `[inferred]` · `[operator]` · `[corroborated]` — same discipline as the
planning notes. Every claim carries how it is known, and nothing inferred gets promoted without
external confirmation.

---

## Why this is a separate document

The planning corpus establishes that execution is deliberately absent from the prep format:

- **Zero stop-losses across 25 prep videos**, six months. But the deep dive discusses stop placement
  directly, so stops exist in the method — they are simply not part of a prep.
- **No size, no R/R** in any prep. The deep dive states a sizing rule outright.
- **The traverse-vs-expansion fork is never verbalized in a prep.** The deep dive gives it as
  mechanical vs emotional, decided live.

So the prep videos define the level set and the branches, and everything about *acting* on them
lives elsewhere. Trade replays are that elsewhere.

## Pre-replay hypotheses, from the existing corpus

Everything here comes from the 25 prep videos and the Job Pivot deep dive — **not** from a replay.
These are the claims the replay analysis exists to confirm, refine, or kill. Listing them first
means the replays get read against a hypothesis set rather than summarized freehand.

### Entry timing

1. **Allow the response to show.** On initiating: *"if you're initiating a position, allow the
   response to show."* Entry follows a reaction at the level, not arrival at it. `[stated]`
2. **Never anticipate.** *"No need to bid… no need to anticipate. Allow it to move, show its
   hand."* `[stated]`
3. **Entry is a pullback into a pre-marked band**, never a chase — the strongest rule in the
   planning corpus and presumably binding here too. `[corroborated, planning]`
4. **Do not enter at a pause point.** At a target mid-move: *"we come down here and we pause and
   we're at the 1B — don't short this. Let it pull back."* The pause is where you gauge absorption
   and look for a scalp against the move, not where you join it. `[stated]`

### Reading the level interaction

5. **Mechanical vs emotional** — does price pause at MGI or accelerate through it. Emotional means
   "no point in countering this." This is the closest thing to an execution-time regime gate.
   `[stated]`
6. **Control follows acceptance, not position.** *"Unless we're building below it, the control is
   not there… if price is below, the control is not above it."* `[stated]`
7. **Absorption at the level** is gauged before acting — repeatedly, at the pivot and at zone edges.
   `[stated]`

### Exhaustion — three named tells

8. **Profile shape** — a spike with volume build, then a traverse back across, leaving a small build
   above. Expect that player back in command, targeting the pivot. `[stated]`
9. **LVN return.** `[stated]`
10. **Leg-to-leg delta "pull stack flip"** — large one-sided delta making no progress, offer
    stepping down, aggressive orders arriving underneath, then acceleration. `[stated]`

### Stops

11. **Structural, not fixed-distance.** *"You should have a look of exhaustion or some sort of
    structural component in order to lean on."* An entry without something to lean on is not a
    trade. `[stated]`
12. **Zone edges are not automatically the stop** — *"we might not be able to set our stop loss
    directly at the top of the balance zone. That's fine."* `[stated]`

### Sizing and patience

13. **Never full size mid-zone.** *"Nobody wants to be full size in the middle of a balance zone. We
    patiently wait to exploit the edges."* `[stated]`
14. **Resting in value with no setup is a pause**, not a trade. `[stated]`
15. **Compression discomfort reduces size** — *"if you're not comfortable with the read, reduce size
    or pause, allow for a move to begin."* `[stated]`

### Management

16. **Reoffer/rebid a zone repeatedly until it stops working** — *"we can reoffer that zone until it
    stops — until you take a stop into that."* Implies a series of attempts at one level, not a
    single shot. `[stated]`
17. **Targets are pause points**, useful as exits or partials whether or not they are drawn.
    `[stated]`

## What replays should settle

Ordered by how much they would change an implementation.

1. **What triggers the actual click.** Every rule above is a filter; none of them is a trigger. The
   replays are the only place the entry moment is visible.
2. **Whether the mechanical/emotional call is made from price or from order flow** — and how early.
   The planning research assumed order flow; the deep dive suggests level response is enough.
   Replays show which he actually uses in the moment.
3. **How a trade is managed after entry** — scaling, partials at targets, moving stops, and what
   makes him exit before a target.
4. **Where stops actually go**, in points, relative to the structure leaned on.
5. **How many attempts** at a level before abandoning it, and what "until it stops" means in
   practice.
6. **What he does when the plan is wrong** — the prep branches cover the market's behaviour, not his
   own error.
7. **Whether execution differs by regime** — mechanical vs emotional, wide vs narrow zone, and in
   the correction-era sessions versus calm ones.
